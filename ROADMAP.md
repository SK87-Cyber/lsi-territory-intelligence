## 1. What we are building, in one paragraph

A continuously updated market-intelligence platform for roadway-lighting work across eight state DOTs (NC, SC, GA, AL, MS, TN, KY, AR). The map is the interface; the intelligence engine is the product. Built from the public record only (owner decision, Sep 1, 2026), it answers the sales questions the public record can answer: Where is the money? Where is the lighting? Who pays for it, and who decides it goes in? Who buys product? What is coming next? Two questions from the original framing are permanently out of its reach and are stated as limits rather than promised: manufacturer share (no state read so far publishes who is installed) and the owner's own customer map (his data, ruled out of the platform). Every number keeps its source and evidence grade. Nothing is manufactured.

### Non-negotiable integrity rules (inherited by every milestone)
- No manufactured geography — project sums are `IDENTIFIED_PROGRAMMED_PROJECT_VALUE`, never "district budget."
- No manufactured lighting dollars — L3/L4 never enter confirmed totals.
- No manufactured awards — apparent low ≠ final award. Item C stamped AWARDED, or Board minutes recording award, are the only confirmations.
- No manufactured manufacturer share — QPL eligibility ≠ installation.
- No manufactured people — a vacancy stays UNKNOWN; emails are never guessed from a naming pattern.
- Every displayed figure traces to a numbered reference.

### ✓ 1.11 Ownership, funding and the decision body — the layer under the bid data
**What it establishes.** Before any bid data exists, one policy decides whether roadway lighting can appear in a DOT contract at all. NCDOT's Roadway Lighting Policy (2020) states that the Department constructs and maintains lighting only at **interchanges and along continuous sections of fully controlled-access roadways** that meet its criteria, tested by a **Priority Index** measured against a **Base Priority Index** and reviewed every two years. Everywhere else, the municipality reimburses the full design and construction cost or agrees to maintain and operate the system — and where a local government fails to maintain a system it agreed to operate, the Department **withholds a portion of that government's Powell Bill funds** equal to the maintenance cost.
**Why it matters more than another letting.** It explains the year's numbers rather than merely reporting them. Only 7 of 134 Central contracts carried lighting and nearly all the product value sat in two high-mast packages, both at freeway locations. That is not a thin sample — it is the policy working exactly as written. **NCDOT-funded lighting is an interchange and freeway product line.** Surface-street lighting is usually municipal money, a different customer on a different budget cycle, and it never appears in an NCDOT letting at all when the town builds it under an encroachment.
**Who decides.** The **Roadway Lighting Committee** makes the final decision on whether lighting goes into a roadway contract — seven voting members chaired by the State Roadway Design Engineer, with an FHWA observer. An Electrical Engineer prepares the lighting evaluation report and the Lighting and Electrical Team Lead submits it. **Four of the seven seats are already named in this model with verified direct emails** (Smallwood as chair, Hall, Lindgren, Brooks); four are identified as seats but not yet as people, and are marked UNKNOWN under rule 5 rather than guessed.

## 2b. Phase 2 — Common data platform, by milestone

### ✓ 2.1 The transferable layer split
**What it does.** The platform is now formally twelve layers, declared once in a `LAYERS` array: seven that transfer to any state DOT and five derived from reading bid tabulations. Three structural changes came with it.
1. **Registries.** Every state's data hangs off an object keyed by state id — `YEAR`, `PIPELINE`, `COMPETE`, `DIVACT`, `DICTS`, `OWNERSHIP`, `DECISION`. Adding a state is filling slots, not editing render code.
2. **`has(state, layer)` replaced `isNC`.** Fourteen hardcoded `s.id==="NC"` branches drove the renderer before this run; six remain and all six are genuine NC presentation (the Division choropleth, the Division chips, the NCDOT contact note). Every *data* branch now asks the layer model.
3. **Coverage is computed, never declared.** `has()` runs a predicate against the data a state actually carries, so the coverage matrix cannot claim a layer the page lacks. The Roadmap tab's layer table is generated from the same array, so the roadmap and the product cannot disagree about what a layer is.
**Honest degradation.** One `missingCard()` helper replaced five bespoke UNKNOWN strings. An absent layer states what it is and what would fill it. It never renders a zero.
**Two things a reader can see.** A coverage matrix on the Overview — eight states × twelve layers, computed — and a "what this model is built from" tile on every state page. Both make the rollout backlog visible: every state except NC is missing exactly *Who pays*, *Who decides* and *Pipeline* among the transferable seven.
**The ownership panel became data.** It renders from `OWNERSHIP[id]` and `DECISION[id]` rather than hand-written HTML, so the next state fills an object instead of writing a panel. That was the actual test of "zero schema changes" and it is the change that makes state two cheap.
**Delivered when:** a state with the seven transferable layers populated and the five bid-derived layers empty renders every view without breaking. — **Done, and enforced.** The seven non-NC states *are* that case: `verify.js` now asserts all eight pages render, that no non-NC state carries a bid-derived layer, and that no page emits `NaN`, `undefined`, `Infinity` or a `$0` where a layer is absent.
